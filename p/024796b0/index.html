Routes Functionality For Saige Real Estate App

During this project, I engaged in dual-track discovery and development, alongside validation efforts. I crafted stepping-stone solutions, gathered insights, and aligned with partner teams. I iterated on early concept solutions through testing and validation, followed by design and development phases. Throughout, I continually gathered insights, allowing for iterative refinement of the process.

About Saige App

The Saige platform serves as a comprehensive toolkit for agents, offering various tools to streamline their workflow. The primary tool, Real Estate Tracker, allows agents to monitor deal details, track progress, and anticipate market trends using AI data. Additionally, agents can order marketing materials such as signs, social media ads, postcards, and brochures through the Data Market tool, simplifying the promotion of their listings.

As a UX designer

I collaborated closely with my team, which included a product manager, business analyst, software development lead, and other key stakeholders. Together, we analyzed user needs and backend requirements to develop new features, including enhancements to agent-to-agent and agent-to-client communication, as well as the implementation of new functionalities such as dashboard, token, and subscription management for new features. During bi-weekly sprints, I worked hand-in-hand with developers to ensure our designs were successfully implemented, providing a seamless user experience.

Research

Discovery - 

To understand the problems, my team and I went into our local Real Estate offices working with us to shadow and conduct small interviews.

User Interviews & Shadowing: What We Learned

  • "It's a bit chaotic, switching between Gmail, Google Maps, Excel sheets, and who knows what else just to get through my day. It's time-consuming and confusing to keep track of everything"

  • "Managing listings feels like a full-time job in itself. Every time I add a new property, it's like a domino effect of updates and changes to make. It's a constant struggle to keep everything organized and up-to-date."

  • “Being able to pan ahead and save routes for future use would be a game-changer. It's frustrating not having that functionality at my fingertips."

Affinity Maps

After identifying specific issues through numerous complaints, I conducted in contextual user interviews with 5 agents to grasp their needs. Following the interviews, I transcribed recordings and assigned different color sticky notes to each participant. Organizing their responses by research objectives, I gained insights by identifying shared pain points, desires, and needs.

Discovered problems

  • There is no consistent tool for route planning: coordinating visit times, and adding locations to show houses

  • Communication spread out across email, Google Maps, and Excel sheets is time-consuming and exhausting.

The High Level Requirements

  • Add a Customizable Route Creation that helps agents pick out houses to show clients and even maps out the best way to see them all in one day.

  • Agents can send email invitations with just one click, coordinating visit times and adding locations to the map for convenient route planning.

"Let's put ourselves into the shoes of product users by storytelling."

User Flow

I used flowcharts to organize my ideas about how the app should work. I broke my flows into some main functions: Login, Add Routes and Start routes.

Wireframes & Testing

I created wireframes that we could show to users to get their gut reaction so we could understand if we were moving in the right direction.

What is Learned

The reaction from the users was overall positive. They felt like this would save them time and consolidate a lot of their work, but we also heard questions like...

  • How do I send a quick message to the opposite-side real estate agent?

  • Will it be manual or easy access to find the contact information?

  • What does "service time" mean?

  • How can I easily change the departure or the service time?

  • Can I schedule a week or days before to put it in my calendar?

  • Can I add all my favorite listings together to visit rather than manually inputting each address?

Solving The Biggest Problems

After getting feedback, I wanted to solve all the problems, but to stay focused and avoid adding too much, we decided to tackle only the biggest and most common ones, which were:

  • Quick message and appointment button for the opposite-side real estate agent.

  • Obvious edit button to change details.

  • A screen to show all created routes for planning ahead and saving routes for future use.

  • Automatically adding by selecting customers' preferences.

Iterations

With Saige user needs at the forefront, we deliberated on how to integrate Favorites listings and Listings Details Data functionalities while enhancing Saige routes features through iterative development cycles.

UI Design

Once the usability issues were resolved, I moved on to design the final screens in Figma. My goal was to create a visual identity that’s aligned with the brand’s values and message, which is: “brand motto”. Also, I’ve checked the competition and took a deep dive into my catalog of references for inspiration.

Problems Solved

Agents can create planned routes for visits on system.

Agents can easily access the the opposite side agents contact and sent text to have appoipment.

Appoipment confirmation is on the system automatically

Results

The Routes was released to 100 agents / 2.7% of total population. Within the first week there was:

46 total requests

35 unique agent customers on platform

22 total repeat customers on platform

Learnings

The project taught me the value of empathy-driven design and the importance of continuously seeking user feedback. It has influenced my approach to design, emphasizing the need for iterative prototyping, user testing, and collaboration throughout the design process.

This project significantly contributed to my growth as a designer by challenging me to think critically, innovate, and adapt to evolving project requirements. It allowed me to hone my problem-solving skills and gain a deeper understanding of user-centered design principles.
