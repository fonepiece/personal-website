ZARA Website Case Study

Improve Zara website UX through redesign and added features, driven by user feedback and market research.

Project Overview

I conducted thorough user research, redesigning Zara's website interfaces to enhance user experience and align with high fashion standards. Analyzing user feedback, I made customer-centric improvements and added functionalities based on market research, significantly enhancing app usability. My goal was to boost Zara's online presence and customer engagement.

Problem

The main challenge was managing Zara's vast inventory for user-friendly navigation. My goal was to create an intuitive layout despite the extensive product range. I referred back to this issue to justify design decisions, ensuring a seamless user experience.

Solution

The project improved Zara's website, enhancing navigation and layout. This led to a smoother shopping experience, higher customer satisfaction, and increased conversions.

Heuristic evaluation

I systematically identify and address usability issues in user interface designs, ensuring a more user-friendly and efficient product.

Task Analysis

I used Zoom meeting for contextual inquiry by conducting semistructured interviews and record keeping to gather data by users shopping experience with Zara and Competitors H&M and Simon's.

  • Task: Find a small size black jacket to the cart.

  • Focused on: Scannability and better ways to sort the inventory on Zara.com

  • Hypothesis: Challenges in scannability and inventory sorting on Zara.com may hinder users' ability to quickly find the desired small size black jacket, potentially causing longer navigation times, increased frustration, and website abandonment.

  • Goal: Enhance user experience by optimizing time efficiency.

Insights From Task Analysis:

  • Random Layout

  • Hidden Navigation Elements. Lack of directions

  • Unintuitive and insufficient filtering options

Personas

I wanted to really get what our users were all about – what they wanted, needed, and how they shopped. So, I created four personas for each user group. I based these on chats and surveys with users and kept updating them as we learned more during the project.

Customer Journey Map

Created a customer journey map to figure out how customers use the service and find areas to make it better. The map showed where users faced issues and where we could make things even better, especially in the consideration and loyalty stages. So, I focused extra on these parts while designing.

Card Sorting

To make sure that the site’s information architecture is aligned with user expectations, I had 10remote card sorting sessions using Optimal Workshop. My goal was to find smaller, intuitive groupings for the existing 36 product categories. After organizing the data, we ended up with 5 clearly defined main categories with 7-7 subcategories in each.

Tree Testing

Before proceeding to create the sketches, I wanted to ensure the sitemap's effectiveness. I had 10 people test it by completing the task I designed. The testing resulted in a 100% completion rate and a 60% faster shopping experience.

Sketches

My sketches and wireframes were all about quickly bringing ideas to life, keeping user insights and business goals in mind. I tweaked layouts to simplify and make things clearer, ensuring smooth navigation. Going back to the sketches helped me stay on track, making sure the design was user-friendly and making steady progress.

Mockups

I translated my initial sketches into mockups using Figma. To enhance them, I incorporated relevant stock images. At this point, the mockups were well-defined, evolving into high-fidelity prototypes. I had previously validated the information architecture through tree testing.

UI Design

Once the usability issues were resolved, I moved on to design the final screens in Figma. My goal was to create a visual identity that’s aligned with the brand’s values and message, which is: “brand motto”.

Open prototype in Figma ↗

Usability Testing -

I created a fully functional, high-fidelity prototype of the new flows using Figma. I conducted Guerrilla testing in the university cafeteria, recruiting 35 volunteers to compare Zara's online order process with my design solution. Surprisingly, participants found no issues with the new design but provided valuable recommendations to enhance the overall ordering process. Specific issues with the cart and other aspects were identified for improvement in subsequent studies.

Next steps

01

To validate this design, extensive testing with substantial sample sizes (over 1000 users) is essential. The evaluation should involve measuring sales volume against the current site, considering Zara's global reach, and accommodating diverse cultures and locations.

02

Additionally, it's crucial to assess user feedback, new completion rates, changes in error frequency, and factors like bounce rates and abandoned carts. These comprehensive tests and analyses are vital to ensure the effectiveness and adaptability of the design across various contexts and user behaviors.

Learnings

This project really changed how I see design. I learned that putting users first and making small tweaks based on what they say is the way to go. It surprised me how simpler designs can actually be way better for users. Now, I'm all about easy navigation, clean layouts, and making users happy. I've become more open to feedback too, knowing it's key to making interfaces people love to use. Overall, it's been a game-changer for my design approach!
